# Context probe pre-generation evaluation

This is a three-arm illustrative counterfactual, not a reconstruction of Washington DOL's actual pre-change capabilities. The prior study and all its inputs, outputs, scores and keys remain unchanged.

Common to Q/R/S: same workflow, same Problem and Medium, same skills/access/tools and normal capacity assumptions, same essential data result/quality/traceability, same permission to consider different divisions of work and ordering within the supplied guarantees. No arm asks to merge or references the historical solution. No new Skill guidance.

Variable: rationale and necessity of the role boundary. Q unknown; R historical equipment placement and no requirement for different performers; S different performers are a protected guarantee. R versus S changes the business condition and expected valid solution set; it is a sensitivity control, not an experiment in which the real business facts changed.

Hypotheses are not predetermined findings. Material boundary information may change candidate direction, confidence, or questions. Q may legitimately return a conditional boundary-changing candidate, other grounded alternatives, or a focused question. R may allow a boundary-changing configuration but is not required to return a specific historical solution. S should preserve different-person performance or return clarification rather than silently abolish it. Alternatives that solve the Problem within each condition count as useful even if they do not match the historical idea.

Evaluate each raw on: (1) reads factual versus hypothetical inputs correctly; (2) separates required result/guarantees from current role split; (3) explains a causal connection to waiting; (4) handles Unknowns without fabricating facts or demanding complete information before any useful proposal; (5) respects the supplied hard boundary; (6) treats candidates as unadopted and returns adoption to responsible humans.

Record boundary-changing direction as explicit candidate / conditional candidate / specific question / absent, independently of validity. For Q, preserve uncertainty about the boundary. For R, confidence may be higher but effects and peak capacity remain unmeasured. For S, a candidate may reorganize coverage or timing while retaining different performers; do not label lack of same-person integration a failure. Reject proposals that assert adoption, actual proven benefit, or make the hypothetical conditions facts about Washington.

Compare Q versus R for how resolving boundary uncertainty changes the output, not as proof that extra facts generally help. Q contains less information than R and R additionally states an historical reason; this is one small factor package about boundary rationale, not isolation of every word. Common assumptions may themselves make boundary change more salient and may already be sufficient for a conditional proposal in Q. Do not compare Q causally with the older A-minimal run: the common input and explicit review scope changed.

No model/no-Skill control, no repeated seeds, no statistical inference. The evaluator knows the hypotheses; declared inputs and hashing do not independently attest runtime settings, filesystem isolation or lack of memorization. One capacity failure before output may be retried once with identical settings. No result-driven regeneration.
