## Marginal effect and interpretation

The baseline already represents the decisive business conditions without a Quality field: its preambles and normal/exception fields are not merely formatting. The treatment makes A's deadline and C's two different business tolerances quicker to scan, and correctly leaves B without Quality. In A, the *prohibition of duplicate payment* is a functional invariant applying to resend and partial-failure retry; it need not be a separate quality category. Repeating it in Quality does not change the source guarantee, but maintaining two copies risks divergence. The same is true for D's unapproved-payment rule. Separate placement can improve navigation only while the rule stays attached to its governing activity and agrees with Procedure/Exception/Result. Treating a separate section as a new requirement, or relocating it so the actual use condition becomes vague, changes meaning rather than merely style.

The treatment is not uniformly better. In C it falsely quotes the report deadline as an aggregation-worker utterance, whereas the note reports that deadline in narrative voice. This is an attribution error, not evidence of a new incident or of a different deadline. In B it reduces the numbered questions from three to two but spends one dense topic on reciprocal near-concurrent booking checks. D's treatment omits the baseline's direct question about which timestamp governs the two-year interval. Counts of numbered topics therefore do not measure noise alone. There is no generic availability/performance/security checklist in any cell. The only asserted real operational incident in this assessment is A's *provided synthetic* delayed payment (`b/A/notes.md:7`); B, C and D supply no actual Problem/Pain.

The preregistered rubric (`work/business-quality/issue-107/PROTOCOL.md:5-11`) is useful, but I disagree with any inference that a separately headed Quality field proves improved correctness or is necessary for check derivation. The baseline A and C drafts give concrete conditions to derive, while treatment C's heading coexists with the attribution defect. Nor should a payroll no-duplicate invariant be scored as a missing independent nonfunctional quality field. I would score the *meaning and reviewability* of each condition first, then its placement and redundancy. The eight cells are one run each: they cannot establish response reliability, causal attribution to guidance for every difference, or effective model and context isolation.

**Recommendation: additional verification before permanent adoption of a Quality field in the Skill or grammar.** A targeted follow-up should test whether human reviewers can locate and distinguish C-like business continuity and reporting deadlines, whether a draft preserves source attribution, and whether duplicate guarantees drift during revision. The *conceptual optionality* can already be used without a grammar, exporter or Skill change: the existing format is recommended rather than mandatory (`docs/adoption.md:59-77`), and the experimental guidance itself says to add the heading only when ordinary fields obscure the condition (`work/business-quality/issue-107/guidance.md:3-5`). A permanent schema or authoring rule is not supported by this small, mixed comparison. Business meaning still requires human confirmation; no Check Items or System Design were authored here.
