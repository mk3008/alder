# Independent audit: pure-Why component supplement

This is a post-hoc, decision-relevant **component supplement**, not a preregistered fifth arm or a new Fresh replication of the original comparison. I read the frozen `pure-why` protocol, treatment, raw response and run metadata, and the originally permitted cases and responses. I did not read investigator conclusions. The treatment and raw files match the SHA-256 hashes and byte counts in `run.json`. The treatment was recorded as published before dispatch at `19ce79e66e397084dae3589f505e9b296484b2da`; the completed raw/metadata were identified as `474769d00a89566788f27fdb73307cf95275dc9c`. Local hash agreement alone does not independently prove publication timing.

## What the treatment isolates

The added paragraph addresses only Activity Why: a concrete business purpose or intended condition, independent of the chosen means and connected to actual work. It contains **no** instruction to stop a candidate-shaped draft when background is missing, ask for a Problem, classify adoption status, route to Optimization, or create a new lifecycle state. In that instructional sense, this is genuinely a Why-quality-only treatment and differs materially from B's compound Why plus intake stop-and-ask rule. Its response could still ask background questions naturally under existing guidance, so behavioral differences cannot be assigned with certainty to any single phrase.

## Comparison and conclusion

The supplement reproduced the original Control's key separation: the changed means was **not falsely labelled agreed** (Control S2/S3 also marked drafts unagreed), yet a reviewable change-shaped response appeared before the missing background was checked. B and C had asked for the Problem or intended condition before presenting changed Activities in S2/S3; the supplement did not. A cleaner Why is therefore **not sufficient in this one sampled six-case batch** to achieve that intake behavior. S3 especially demonstrates why a means-independent Why does not by itself validate a two-stage business structure. This does not prove B's stop rule is uniquely necessary, that a future model will behave the same way, or that B/C are product-ready. The S4–S6 controls show no major forced re-justification from this treatment, but include the representation and terminology caveats above.

The supplement is post-hoc after the original outputs and audit were inspected, uses the earlier Control rather than a concurrent new Control, and has one extra agent context answering the six cases in fixed order. This is one observation per cell within one context, with possible within-batch carryover and stochastic/time variation. The six synthetic cases may favor the tested distinction; no user answers, business adoption, documents or code were observed. The recorded `gpt-6-sol` / `medium` settings were requested, not independently attested; token and billing costs are unavailable. The source packet was explicitly handed to the responder, so this cannot establish automatic plugin routing or production behavior. No additional responder runs or product changes are warranted by this audit alone.
