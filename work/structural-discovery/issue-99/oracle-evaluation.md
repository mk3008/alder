## Discovery against the sealed oracle

**Recall and root structural match: strong.** `discovery-raw.md` section 1 identifies the shared numbered supply, ledger commitments over resident intervals, class type/count fixed before individual numbering, absent settled application-time cross-check, Friday-noon school demand, and no agreed cross-business allocation point. Its question “at what exact event does a numbered set cease being available to another use?” and its demand view by interval, count and number closely match the oracle's commitment boundary. Section 2 expands the proposed-to-numbered transition and section 9 tests the Wednesday/Friday/Saturday timing. This is a substantive temporal capacity and commitment hypothesis, not a generic request to integrate records or a mere missing-field observation. The raw independently notices the school custody interval and does not demand a particular software system or an invented priority.

**Grounding and uncertainty: good, with a few weaker extensions.** Most present-state claims can be traced to named v4 activities, objects and unresolved items. It clearly separates “Present structure,” “Opportunity hypothesis,” “Unknowns,” and human-verifiable questions, and explicitly disclaims observed delays, clashes, losses, frequencies and benefits. It does not claim a quantified shortfall or assume ordinary and school classes occur on the same Saturday. In section 1, the phrase “no settled common allocation point” is a reasonable inference from the two distinct stages and open priority/approval questions, not direct testimony that staff have never coordinated informally. Section 3's suggestion of reconciling shelf, tag and maintenance record is grounded in v4 open item 4; section 4's return custody and section 5's manifest are exploratory extensions of open handoffs, not established incidents. Section 6 draws attention to a notice with no specified input; useful but more peripheral to the oracle. Section 7's shared record relation similarly risks reading as generic integration if detached from the time and numbered-asset evidence in sections 1–2. Neither displaces the central finding or asserts an existing problem as measured fact.

**False positives and negative control.** No material false positive or recommended elimination/merger of existing checks appears. Section 8 treats class completion and maintenance release as separate decisions; sections 3 and 7 also preserve distinct records. It does not explicitly articulate the oracle negative control's entire distinction between the resident handover check (mutual condition/accessory and return accountability), instructor's pre-use teaching setup/protective equipment/workspace check, and maintenance functional inspection. The raw mentions the resident signature and instructor check separately and never concludes that either inspection is redundant. Thus it **passes the prohibition on merging**, while earning only partial credit for affirmative explanation of why both use-time checks remain necessary. The raw's attention to school custody and school-facing notice is additive investigation rather than an unsupported claim of defective operations.

**Human-verifiable questions: strong.** Sections 1, 2 and 9 ask the owner to reconstruct a real week of promised periods, counts and numbered assets, identify the exact commitment point, test usable inventory and readiness at each milestone, and determine decision and notification authority. The final decision questions ask about priority, substitution, change approval, affected contacts, school securing and release. Staff could answer by examining the ledger, schedule, inspection sheet, maintenance record and actual tools, then making explicit business decisions. The questions appropriately remain open rather than certifying the hypothesis or imposing a policy.
