# Draft Alder Business Design

Use the bundled, byte-identical [authoring and adoption guidance](references/adoption.md#1-place-business-design-where-the-agent-can-read-it) as the authority for business meaning and authoring. Read its whole section 1, through the heading for section 2, including its authoring/correlation guidance. For the supported optional Business Graph Markdown profile, also read [the bundled format specification](references/business-graph.md#opt-in-markdown-profile-v1) through its minimal example. These are copies from one pinned Alder revision, recorded with digests in [provenance.json](references/provenance.json); do not substitute examples or this routing skill for the guidance. Project-specific instructions may select an existing format or path. The export profile is optional, and export success does not establish business correctness.

1. Read the provided notes and the product's `AGENTS.md`, established Business Design, and relevant project constraints. Identify the requested business boundary and the target file from project instructions or the established layout; otherwise use `docs/business-design/`. If multiple plausible targets or incompatible source accounts remain, ask a focused question before overwriting an existing design. A new draft may record uncertainty without waiting for every answer.
2. Separate explicit source facts, reasonable *structural* organization of those facts, and business meaning that needs human judgment. Keep the requester's business language. Draft the known flow, roles, objects, actual information exchanges, normal outcomes, and explicitly stated exceptions following the bundled guide and any established project format. Do not copy unconfirmed details from an example or existing implementation into an agreed requirement. In the optional export profile, use its exact visible headings and references; do not put extra unsupported headings after the preamble. Mark the entire new document as a draft pending review.
3. Record concrete open questions in a clearly marked **unconfirmed** section of the draft, in the title/preamble when using the optional export profile. Locate each question by affected activity or object and say which observable outcome, responsibility, or handoff needs a decision. If a required field is unknown, use explicit provisional wording or a neutral placeholder and include the corresponding question; do not silently fill in policy, permissions, timing, cardinality, or failure behavior. Distinguish source contradictions from missing facts. Avoid speculative questions that do not affect the described work.
4. Walk the drafted work from each participant's perspective and check Input → Procedure → Output, Result → subsequent When, and any Exception → receiving Exception When against the source notes. Before writing, check **every declarative statement** in Procedure, Result, Object Information, and explanatory prose against the supplied source. A natural-sounding consequence is still a business policy if it changes availability, authorization, state, or the next action. For example, “a cancelled reservation immediately frees its time slot” cannot be asserted merely because cancellation was mentioned; ask what cancellation does to availability. Move unsupported consequences to the unconfirmed questions and leave the established result no stronger than the source. Write only the requested Business Design file(s), preserving unrelated content when updating an existing design. Report the path, source material used, main open questions, and what still needs human confirmation. Do not label the draft agreed or derive Check Items from it.

This skill authors Business Design only. Do not implement the product, generate Check Items, run Optimization Review, perform implementation review, or edit unrelated files. The separate `alder-review-implementation` skill remains read-only. Include `Alder plugin 0.2.1` and the pinned authoring source revision from provenance in the result; identify a moving plugin checkout's source commit when known.
