# Draft Alder Business Design

Use the bundled, byte-identical [authoring and adoption guidance](references/adoption.md#1-place-business-design-where-the-agent-can-read-it) as the authority for business meaning and authoring. Read its whole section 1, through the heading for section 2, including its authoring/correlation guidance. For the supported optional Business Graph Markdown profile, also read [the bundled format specification](references/business-graph.md#opt-in-markdown-profile-v1) through its minimal example. These are copies from one pinned Alder revision, recorded with digests in [provenance.json](references/provenance.json); do not substitute examples or this routing skill for the guidance. Before drafting, also read the [bundled document structure](../alder-review-business-design/references/business-design-structure.ja.md), whose separate source revision and digest are recorded in its [provenance](../alder-review-business-design/references/provenance.json). The adoption guide’s version-pinned web links provide further context; the required structure is available locally in this installed package. Project-specific instructions may select an existing format or path. The export profile is optional, and export success does not establish business correctness.

1. Read the provided notes and the product's `AGENTS.md`, established Business Design, and relevant project constraints. Identify the requested business boundary and the target file from project instructions or the established layout; otherwise use `docs/business-design/`. If multiple plausible targets or incompatible source accounts remain, ask a focused question before overwriting an existing design. A new draft may record uncertainty without waiting for every answer.
2. Before expanding a feature-, means- or flow-shaped change request, apply the bundled guide's “Before drafting a proposed business change” boundary. Reuse supplied background and settled decisions; ask only for missing context that could change a still-open choice. If candidate versus decided status is genuinely unclear, ask briefly rather than assume either. Keep any Optimization Review separate from this authoring task. Continue the known draft with deferrable or explicitly undecided matters visible instead of blocking it until every question is settled. Separate explicit source facts, reasonable *structural* organization of those facts, and business meaning that needs human judgment. Keep the requester's business language. Draft the known flow, roles, objects, actual information exchanges, normal outcomes, and explicitly stated exceptions following the bundled guide and any established project format. Do not copy unconfirmed details from an example or existing implementation into an agreed requirement. In the optional export profile, use its exact visible headings and references; do not put extra unsupported headings after the preamble. Mark the entire new document as a draft pending review.
3. Record concrete open questions in a clearly marked **unconfirmed** section of the draft, in the title/preamble when using the optional export profile. Locate each question by affected activity or object and say which observable outcome, responsibility, or handoff needs a decision. If a required field is unknown, use explicit provisional wording or a neutral placeholder and include the corresponding question; do not silently fill in policy, permissions, timing, cardinality, or failure behavior. Distinguish source contradictions from missing facts. Avoid speculative questions that do not affect the described work. Identify which answers would change current business meaning, correlations, responsibility, or conditions; defer details that do not affect these. Where multiple activities independently promise or update use of the same Object, finite resource, or business state, check **each direction at the earliest commitment**: when one activity first reserves a count, time, or named item for future use, does it recognize commitments already made by the other, and vice versa? A later choice of concrete items does not answer whether the earlier count or time commitment considered the other activity. If either direction is unknown and materially affects the result, ask in the response to the requester **whether the actor actually checks the other activity's recorded future commitments at that earliest point**, identifying the relevant record and time. Do not replace this with only “does the actor consider the other commitment?”: that can assume the actor already knows of it. Ask the reciprocal question where its check is also unknown, then check any later assignment or change and how it reaches affected parties. Apply this only where the actual work calls for it, not as a checklist for every relation.
4. Walk the drafted work from each participant's perspective and check Input → Procedure → Output, Result → subsequent When, and any Exception → receiving Exception When against the source notes. In both drafting and revision, apply the bundled document structure's “原要求の保持と欄への割当” check to the document-level Scope and every Activity name, separately from checking that source facts were retained. Before writing, check **every declarative statement** in Procedure, Result, Object Information, and explanatory prose against the supplied source. Access to or visibility of information does not establish that a person routinely checks or uses it in that Activity; when the difference changes business meaning, ask rather than turn access into a current Procedure. A natural-sounding consequence is still a business policy if it changes availability, authorization, state, or the next action. For example, “a cancelled reservation immediately frees its time slot” cannot be asserted merely because cancellation was mentioned. Even “when the cancelled slot becomes available” presupposes that it becomes available: if this is unknown, ask **whether** it becomes available before asking when. Move unsupported consequences and their presuppositions to the unconfirmed questions and leave the established result no stronger than the source. Write only the requested Business Design file(s), preserving unrelated content when updating an existing design.
5. Apply the bundled guide's [review and revision loop](references/adoption.md#review-and-revise-a-draft-before-returning-it). Run the existing [read-only Business Design review](../alder-review-business-design/SKILL.md) in a separate reviewer agent or fresh context when available and useful, supplying the current draft, original sources/revisions, requested boundary and unresolved questions. Without a separate reviewer, perform a distinct read-only review phase in the same agent and report that it was not independent. In either case, the reviewer must read that skill and its required guidance, perform the review and return findings; merely requesting a review is not completion. Do not replace this with step 4's authoring self-check or graph export. If the review itself cannot run, return the available draft with review pending and the concrete limitation, not a passing result.
6. Compare each actual finding with its source evidence and apply the bundled loop’s three classes: uniquely repairable error, clear problem with an undecided answer, or optional proposal/hypothesis. Detecting an error does not determine its correct repair. Repair supported omissions or distortions and wording/structure defects grounded in the applicable guidance or explicit requester requirements only when the repair is uniquely determined without adding business facts; preserve missing facts as concrete questions; retain ambiguous or contradictory interpretations as unapproved candidates. Put material undecided Who, When/Procedure contradictions and step/I/O gaps in the draft’s unconfirmed preamble and the response. Return only concretely useful optional proposals in the conversational/console result, allowing zero; do not add unaccepted proposals to Business Design, unconfirmed questions, Findings, Scope or Decision Records. Do not invent I/O, records, rules or operational Problem / Pain to satisfy a finding. Keep known states and triggers distinct from unknown transmission/storage. Record the location, evidence, correction or question and remaining status briefly in the existing exchange, not a new required artifact or business-text review log.
7. After correction, run the read-only review again on the revised draft and evidence. Check the affected findings/correlations and the whole requested boundary. Continue while source-supported corrections make progress. Stop when no source-supported correction remains, when substantially the same finding persists without improvement or new evidence, or when review failure, unavailability or runtime/context limits prevent completion. Report the last version actually reviewed, unreviewed changes and unresolved findings on incomplete review; never call a failed or unperformed re-review “no problems found”. Keep the reviewer read-only and business agreement with people.
8. Present the draft and ask the requester a **small number of the highest-value material questions in the response**, not only in the file. If answers arrive within this requested authoring task, revise the existing design and recheck affected correlations. After a standalone review, require a separate explicit editing request before applying human acceptance, rejection or deferral; a decision alone does not authorize a write. Keep unanswered or explicitly undecided matters visible for human review; stop with a reviewable draft when the requester cannot decide now, the detail is out of scope or deferrable, or another question has little value. After an explicit “not decided yet,” acknowledge and summarize the pending decisions as statements; do not restate them as a new list of questions or solicit the same answers in the immediate response. Ask only genuinely new material questions if the answer exposes a different necessary decision. Do not keep questioning until every detail is filled, infer a business decision from silence, or call the draft agreed. Report the path, source material used, grounded corrections, actual review coverage and stopping reason, questions asked, deferred decisions, and what still needs human confirmation. Keep review mechanics and evidence history separate from the Business Design text. Do not derive Check Items from the draft.

Do not register all unconfirmed matters as Findings. For requested structured findings in the optional Viewer profile, follow [the Findings contract](references/business-graph.md#optional-findings-annotations). Preserve review finding IDs, evidence, related exact Activity/Object names, and open questions when incorporating authorized review output. Use `question` for missing/undecided business facts and `correction` for supported defects; keep actual business Problem / Pain level separate. Do not invent targets or evidence to populate a badge. Unassigned and document-wide findings remain explicit. Set `resolved` only after checking that the stated defect has been repaired or the relevant human answer establishes resolution. A repaired wording issue does not resolve an unanswered business question. This optional section records findings; it does not approve business meaning.

This skill authors Business Design only. Do not implement the product, generate Check Items, run Optimization Review, perform implementation review, or edit unrelated files. The separate `alder-review-implementation` skill remains read-only. Include the installed Alder version from `../../plugin.json` and the pinned authoring source revision from provenance in the result; identify a moving plugin checkout's source commit when known.
